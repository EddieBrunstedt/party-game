Lessons from this session (Lantern Run):
- **Forces fight the controllers you already built.** The grapple's tow did nothing at first: each glider's own return to cruising speed (9 units a second²) cancelled it, and the rope's sideways pull was cancelled by steering (which holds a velocity with up to 18 units a second² of thrust). A rope now sets both speeds while it's on and drags positions directly. When adding a force, check what else is steering the same quantity.
- **Ask what decides outs, and when.** The first tuning looked fine by match length, but a probe counting each out's cause (crash, hooked, rammed, other) and time showed outs at 6-10 s, mostly from hooks. That's what led to the canyon warm-up, the weaker tow and the longer cooldown.
- **A camera rule can quietly decide an asymmetric format.** In 1v3 the canyon push (meant to give people time to learn) kept dragging the three back into hook range, so the barge never escaped; with the push off they died together at the cave's mouth. Following the three with the camera and putting the barge out ahead fixed both.
- **Rubber-band the solo by distance, not time.** A barge whose speed depends on how close the nearest chaser is keeps every match's fight near the end; no fixed speed or head start balanced all three difficulties.
- **When contact causes most crashes, skill stops showing.** Between good bots most crashes came from rams, bumps and tows, so a Hard bot barely beat Normals (29%) until Normal bots were made to crash into things on their own too. Count crashes by cause before tuning bot skills.
- **A straggler push must stay inside the window.** Pushing a glider further ahead than the camera's window put it past the leader, and they leapfrogged every tick (a test caught it at time-up).
- **A narrow lens from further back** (26° FOV) keeps a chase camera's near and far gliders at nearly one size; obstacles between the camera and the kill line must be dropped or faded, or they fill the screen.
- **Give players a direct control for the thing they're judged on.** Lantern Run judged you by distance but had no "faster" button, only indirect tools (slipstream, boost, grapple); the developer's first try ended in "how do I go forward?". A probe of a player who steers well but ignores the tools (lost every match) would have caught it before shipping. Then check the simple strategy doesn't dominate: holding the new throttle down forever beat the bots until it cost steering.
- Harness: `rm -rf` on a variable path is blocked by a safety check; write screenshots into fresh directories instead.

Lessons from the session before (Lantern Flail):
- **A slack rope gives a person nothing to work with.** Pure rope physics only swung the lantern when the folk ran big slow circles (0.4 rev/s); circling the stick at a natural 1 rev/s or faster left the hand within reach of the lantern, the rope never went taut, and nothing moved. Gravity (a pendulum pull back under the hand) helped only up to about 0.8 rev/s. The fix was a "whirl": the stick's turning rate pushes the lantern round, up to a top speed, as an arm swings a rope. Measure what a control does across the speeds a person might use (a throwaway that circles the stick at 0.4-2 rev/s) before building bots on it.
- **Bots that read "which way is it turning" from a relative velocity flip-flop**: their own running reverses it. Use the thing's own velocity, and give each bot a fixed default per match.
- **Partners need one shared frame.** Each bolas bot measured "the line to my partner", so their "forward" pointed opposite ways and they stood still.
- **Sweeps leave constants behind.** A `sed` loop over three configs left the last one (giant goal 8) in the source, and all later tuning ran on it; a test pinning the goals caught it. Reset constants after a sweep.
- **A fixed prop layout can be a seat bias.** P3 trailed by 3% in four-player Normal matches over three seed ranges; a barrel always started beside P3's spot. Barrels placed from the seed fixed it.
- **Defence is a bot skill too.** Hard bots in the 1v3 trio were no better than Normal until bots could dodge a fast rival lantern; then the trio's skill counted and the 1v3 curve could be flattened.
- **Coprime goals end ties at time-out** (8 and 3: equal shares only at 0-0), and sudden death took ties from 25-30% to about 5% elsewhere.

Lessons from the session before (Lantern Masquerade):
- **Bots with perfect memory make a deduction game a bloodbath.** The simulation keeps a record of public clues (who stood under a ringing bell, who walked oddly, who struck), and bots that used all of it struck players 1786 times to strollers' 20 in 500 matches. Model what a person can follow: clues fade after a while, and linking two rings to one folk succeeds with a chance that falls with the time between them.
- **Fixed thresholds over regular bot timings act as switches, again.** A 6 s and a 6.5 s tracking window gave 35 and 211 rounds ended by strikes; seeker catch thresholds of 35 and 45 gave the seeker 66% and 4%. Replace the threshold with a chance (fading tracking, a seeker boldness rolled per suspect every 2 s) and the curves go smooth.
- **Check mixed difficulties, not just all-equal matches.** All-equal reports looked fine, but one Easy bot beat three Normal 42% of the time: hurrying is conspicuous, and nobody punished it fast enough, so it just won the bell race. Easy is now slow as well as conspicuous.
- **A round that ends faster than you think:** the first version's rounds lasted 13-22 s. The market's size, the walking pace, strollers gathering at bells, and a 1 s hold to ring all went in before rounds reached ~30 s.
- Still true: an unlit material draws its base colour, so a black base with an emissive draws nothing (the bell markers and the sight vanished at first).

Lessons from the session before (Puzzle Buzzer):
- **Proposing one minigame with draft rules and numbered open questions worked.** The developer answered "make your suggested game, with your suggested rules" in one line. Put a recommended answer in every question so that reply is enough.
- **Asymmetric formats drift with skill when speed is the edge.** Three answering against one are likelier to have the quickest answer, and the quicker everyone is the more that counts: the solo's chance fell 16 points from Easy to Hard whatever the goals. A head start for the solo, sized as a share of the puzzle, flattened it. Check the curve across difficulties before tuning the goals.
- **A fixed delay only bites once it overlaps the bots' timing spread**, and then it bites hard. If bots' times are tightly spread (±30%), any threshold rule behaves like a switch. Wider spreads (±45%) are closer to people and make balance smooth.
- **A seat lead that shows in every difficulty's report can still be seed noise**: `tools bots` uses the same seeds at each difficulty, so the matches share their luck. Count something with more samples (puzzles won per seat) or try another seed range before hunting a bias.
- **The UI font also lacks × and −** (on top of ° ● ○). Use `x` and `-`.

Lessons from two sessions before (Triple Jump):
- **Ask which minigame, then talk the rules through before building.** The developer turned down the offered list and asked for a button-mashing game "not only button mashing", then named Track & Field's triple jump and asked to discuss first. A plain-text proposal with numbered open questions worked better than AskUserQuestion for that: the developer answered each in a line, added the angle from the original and asked for asymmetric 2v2 and 1v3 twists ("not a fan of simply adding stuff together").
- **Fixed targets in 1v3 can't fit every skill level** (Lantern Derby's finish, now Triple Jump's qualifying line). Make the target come from play at the same skill: Triple Jump's solo jumps first and its mark is the line. Make the solo's effect a share, not an amount (the headwind takes a share of the speed).

- **Smooth anything sampled at a moment.** Each press added 7% speed at once, so the take-off speed depended on how recently you'd pressed. The runner's speed now follows the flywheel over 8 ticks.
- **The UI font has no degree sign** (nor ●/○, found last time): "39°" drew "39▯". Check new symbols in a screenshot.
- **An unlit additive material draws its base colour**, not its emissive; with base alpha 0 it draws nothing.

- **Seed bot rolls by mixing the parts, not XORing them.** `seed ^ window` lets match seeds and windows cancel, so matches share each seat's luck and `tools bots` shows seat bias that isn't real (Tabletop Rally's `bot::mix`). Other minigames' bots XOR seed, seat and counters too; if one shows an odd seat skew, check that first.
- **Mesh quads must be wound counter-clockwise seen from above**, or Bevy culls them and the floor silently vanishes.
- **1v3 balance drifts when the arena or the bots' rolls change.** Rerun the 1v3 report after any layout, rule or bot edit.
- Still true: balance 1v3 at every difficulty; throwaway probes as `examples/` (delete before committing); don't run `cargo fmt --all` (lantern-echo isn't rustfmt-clean), use `cargo fmt -p <crate>`.

## How to test saves

- Couch: `./target/debug/game --board --save FILE --rounds 3 --humans 0 --seed 1` saves at each turn start (`saved the game in round N, checksum X`). Then `./target/debug/game --save FILE` and press Space (Continue), Space (join), Enter (start): look for `resumed the saved game in round N, checksum X`.
- Online: `--host --resume --save FILE` and `--join 127.0.0.1:7777`; the client logs `joined the host's board at round N, checksum X`.
- `--resume` without `--host` skips to the couch join screen with the save.
- Never run dev windows without `--save` from the title: they use the real save in `~/.local/share/party-game/board.save`.

## How to test online on one PC

- Two windows: `cargo build -p game`, then `./target/debug/game --host --rounds 2` and `./target/debug/game --join 127.0.0.1:7777` (`--net-delay 30`, `--net-loss 5` for a bad link). Headless: wrap each in `timeout -k 10 <secs> gamescope --backend headless -w 1280 -h 800 -W 1280 -H 800 -- ...`, start the host about 2 s before the client, and script players with `--press SECS:KEY` / `--press-every SECS:Space`.
- Item 7's kill-and-restart recipe and item 6's scripted pad are in `git log -p docs/HANDOFF.md`.

## Gotchas

- Screenshots: `--format duel` with the default `--players 4` is the duel with two benched seats. `--humans 1` holds the instruction card up, which is how to screenshot it.
- Under gamescope the game runs as `./target/debug/game` (a relative path), so `pgrep -f "^$PWD/target/debug/game"` doesn't find it. Use `pgrep -af "target/debug/gam[e]"` (the bracket keeps the pattern from matching your own shell).
- Don't `pkill -f target/debug/game` from the Bash tool: the pattern matches the tool's own shell command.
- The Bash tool runs bash, though the developer's shell is fish.
- bincode isn't self-describing: change anything inside `Game`, `Board` or `Save` and `the_encoding_is_pinned` fails. Bump `SAVE_VERSION`, then re-pin.

## Decisions

- One save slot, written automatically between turns by the couch or the online host; removed at game over.
- A resumed game's seats keep their characters; players pick which seat with left/right.
- Rejoining after a disconnect is by position, not identity (item 7).
