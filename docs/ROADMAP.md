# Roadmap

The single source of truth for **what's done and what's next**. Work items top to bottom. Each item is done when its **Done when** line holds. Then tick it, note anything surprising under it, and update `DESIGN.md` if the design changed.

## Minigame completion levels

- **Playable:** rules complete and fun, bots at all difficulties, `check_all` passing, and placeholder visuals that always read clearly (who is who, what just happened, who is winning). New minigames stop here.
- **Polished:** real characters and animations, sound, particles and juice, an instruction card, Fluent strings. This is done in batched polish passes once the shared systems (characters, audio, particles, UI framework) exist, so nothing is built twice.

A fun problem found at the playable stage gets fixed straight away; polish won't rescue a dull game.

- [x] **Lantern Push** (to playable). Sumo knockoff on a shrinking platform. Introduces:
  - custom deterministic physics in `sim-core` (circles, knockback, ring-out) with `f32` + `math` wrappers + `hash_f32`
  - render interpolation between ticks in `game`
  - Done when: `check_all` passes, bots at all difficulties produce close matches, and screenshots show a readable arena.
  - Rules: A dashes (knockback + stun on whoever it hits), B braces (×4 mass for up to 0.75 s; a dash into a brace rebounds and stuns the attacker). The platform holds at radius 7 for 8 s, then shrinks to 2.2 by 45 s; 75 s time limit, survivors share the win. Supports FFA, 1v3, 2v2 and duel.
  - Evidence: `check_all` (6 seeds in the unit test, 32 via `tools check`), 12 rule tests. 100 bot runs per difficulty at 4 players: avg 42–46 s, wins spread 21–33 per seat. Over 200 seeds: 1v3 solo wins 42% (Easy), 56% (Normal), 34% (Hard); 2v2 is even; one Hard bot beats three Easy bots 57% of the time.

  - Decision: the platform is a disc scaled to the exact simulated radius, with a glowing edge ring, because readability beats voxel purity: decorative voxel tiles crumble off only once fully outside it.
  - Decision: the 1v3 solo is bigger and heavier (radius 0.58, mass 1.35) instead of scoring differently, because in sumo the handicap has to be physical. Tuned with bots; first values (1.6 mass) made the solo win 75%.
  - Decision: `check_all` now runs every supported format (1v3 and 2v2 at 4 players, duel at 2), and `sim-core` gained `Format::team` and `Outcome::from_team_scores`.
  - Interpolation: `ActiveRun` keeps `previous` (the state one tick back), and views blend by `Time<Fixed>::overstep_fraction()`.
  - Surprise: the first knockback values ended games in ~7 s (one hit from the middle was a ring-out), and the next set timed out most games. Bots also need their "won't dash off the edge" check to account for a dash stopping on contact, or they stall on a small platform.
  - Needs human playtest: dash/brace feel, knockback strength, whether the 8 s before shrinking and ~40 s matches drag with 2 players (2-player games mostly end when the platform is nearly minimum size). `cargo run -p game -- --minigame lantern-push --players 4 --humans 1` (add `--format 1v3` or `--format 2v2`).

## Milestone 2: Online alpha

The same content over the network: friends join by IP (over Tailscale or ZeroTier until Steam), mixed couch + online, with reconnects and saves. See ADRs 0002–0004. Broken down with the developer on 2026-10-20. Minigames are a full mesh (every machine reaches every other machine, as `ggrs` needs); the board phase goes through the host.

- [x] **`net` crate: transport.** A small `Transport` trait carrying unreliable datagrams (for `ggrs`) and a reliable, ordered channel (for board messages: our own small ack-and-resend layer over the same UDP socket). A UDP backend, an in-memory backend for tests, and the `ggrs` `NonBlockingSocket` adapter. A handshake carries the game version and a protocol version, so mismatched builds refuse to connect.
  - Done when: tests pass messages between two localhost UDP sockets and between in-memory peers with injected loss, reordering, duplication and delay, and the reliable channel delivers everything once and in order.
  - Built (2026-10-20): `crates/net`. `Transport` moves datagrams (`UdpTransport`, `MemoryTransport` on a `MemoryNetwork`); `Faulty<T>` wraps any transport and loses, duplicates, delays and reorders from a seed. `Endpoint<T>` (cloneable handle, one per machine) does the handshake (`Hello` with a magic, `PROTOCOL_VERSION` and the game version → `Welcome` or `Refuse`), a reliable ordered channel per peer (`send`, then `NetEvent::Message` from `poll`), and `ggrs_socket()`, the `NonBlockingSocket` adapter that only talks to connected peers. Time comes from `Clock` (real, or manual for tests).
  - Reliable channel (`net/src/reliable.rs`): 1 KiB fragments, a 256-fragment window, cumulative acks plus a 32-fragment bitmask, resend timeout from smoothed round trips (40 ms–2 s, doubling on timeout, Karn's rule). Frames are `bincode` with an 8 KiB size limit, so a corrupt length can't make a peer allocate.
  - Evidence: 17 tests. 100–200 messages each way (empty up to 5 KB, so multi-fragment) arrive exactly once and in order over memory and localhost UDP, clean and with 25% loss, 10% duplication, 30 ms delay and 0–40 ms jitter on both ends, plus 60% loss over memory. Also: a different build is refused with the host's versions, a non-hosting peer refuses, three peers connecting to each other at once all connect (the minigame mesh), messages sent mid-handshake arrive, and two real `ggrs` sessions sync through the adapter over a lossy link and see each other's inputs on the right frames. Switching resends off fails every lossy test. 15 repeated runs, no flakes.
  - Surprise: even with no injected faults, a 200-message burst over localhost UDP loses datagrams (the kernel's socket buffer overflows), so resends matter on loopback too.
  - Decision: our own reliability layer rather than a crate, so board messages and `ggrs` share one socket and port (one thing to open or forward), and it's ~200 lines.
  - Decision: everyone listens or connects to everyone; `EndpointConfig::listen` only decides whether hellos from peers we didn't contact are accepted. Who may actually join is the lobby's call (item 3).
  - Left for "Network disconnects": there are no timeouts or heartbeats yet. An unanswered hello repeats every 250 ms forever, and a silent peer stays "connected".
- [x] **Rollback driver.** `net::rollback` drives any `Run<M>` through a `ggrs` `P2PSession`: several local players per peer, 2 frames of input delay, at most 8 frames of rollback. Bots run on the host as its local players. Fixes board bots hashing `usize` lengths (hash as `u64`).
  - Done when: `tools net-check <minigame>` runs 2–4 headless peers over localhost UDP with simulated latency and loss for every minigame, every confirmed frame's checksum matches across peers, and all peers reach the same `Outcome`.
  - Built (2026-10-20): `net::rollback::Rollback<M, A>` wraps a `ggrs` `P2PSession` around a `Run<M>` (save = clone + checksum, load = restore, advance = `Run::step`), 60 Hz, `INPUT_DELAY` 2, `MAX_PREDICTION` 8, `ggrs` desync detection every 30 ticks. Every seat has a handle; `SeatOwner::Local` covers a machine's players, the bots it runs and empty seats (the host's). `update(local_input)` once per tick returns an `Advance` (new tick or not, ticks replayed); `take_confirmed()` gives `(tick, checksum)` for ticks no rollback can change, and `confirmed_outcome()` the outcome once its tick is confirmed, identical on every machine. Events: synchronized, interrupted, resumed, disconnected, desync.
  - `net::check::play_online` is the headless online bar: N endpoints in a full mesh over any transport with `Faulty` links, seats dealt round-robin (so machines get several local players), bots on every seat, all peers stepped round-robin on one thread against a manual clock. `tools net-check <id>` runs it over localhost UDP for every seed, player count and format `check_all` covers, at every peer count from 2 to the player count (defaults: 2 seeds, 40 ms delay, 0–30 ms jitter, 5% loss, 2% duplication).
  - Evidence: `net-check` passes for all three minigames: 64 matches, every confirmed tick compared (Quick Draw 6.5 s, Lantern Push 13.5 s, Firefly Catch 16.6 s for 2 seeds). Lantern Push and Firefly Catch roll back thousands of times per match, never more than 7 ticks. Breaking load-on-rollback makes it fail at tick 210 (`ggrs`' desync detection). `net/tests/rollback.rs` plays each minigame at 2–4 peers over a lossy in-memory network in CI and requires rollbacks on every machine.
  - Fix: the `usize` hashing was in `sim_core::StableHasher`, not the bots: `Vec` lengths went through the default `write_usize` (4 bytes on 32-bit, 8 on 64-bit). It now always hashes 8 bytes, which is what 64-bit builds did already, so no checksum changed (a test pins one).
  - Surprise: in `ggrs` 0.13, reaching the prediction limit isn't an error. `advance_frame` still returns the rollback's requests but skips the new tick, so one update can replay ticks without advancing.
  - Not yet: the game doesn't use the driver (item 5), and peer disconnects only surface as events (item 7).
